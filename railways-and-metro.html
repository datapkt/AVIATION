Planning

Pre-Construction Survey

Before a new line, yard or station is designed, we survey the candidate sites and corridors from the air to check whether the ground is suitable and what lies in the way, tied to DGPS control so the data can be designed on.

Detailed project report

A railway DPR needs reliable base data: topography, existing features, land and quantities. We provide the survey and mapping that the DPR is built on.

Surveying and Mapping

Photogrammetry or LiDAR, tied to DGPS ground control, gives topographic maps and 3D models of the corridor, showing terrain and obstacles before the alignment is fixed.

Site Inspection

Repeat flights record progress, safety hazards and adherence to drawings, with dated imagery shared with the railway and the contractor.

Earthwork Monitoring

Stockpile volumes and cut and fill quantities measured from each survey, so earthwork can be checked against the schedule.

Route Optimization

For a new alignment we map each option with an orthomosaic, elevation model and contours, check conflicts with existing structures, and digitise revenue maps to estimate land cost alongside construction quantities.

Construction

Project progress monitoring

During construction, regular aerial surveys keep the railway, the contractor and the consultant looking at the same up-to-date picture of the site, so delays and deviations are caught early.

  • Regular aerial surveys catch delays and deviations early.
  • Flights are planned to follow railway safety rules on and near the track.
  • Video reports highlight unfinished work such as incomplete overbridge piers.

Inspection

Railway bridges and buildings

Railway bridges and structures are hard and sometimes unsafe to inspect from the ground. Drones photograph them in detail without putting staff at risk, and our GIS team turns the imagery into 3D models and maps for structural assessment.

  • Bridges and structures inspected in detail without staff working at height.
  • Lower cost and risk than traditional access methods.
  • 3D models and maps for structural assessment.
Railway track

For scheduled inspection of busy track, drones collect data quickly while following railway safety rules. The imagery is processed into orthoimagery, 3D models, point clouds and textured meshes, used to measure track and spot hazards early.

  • Data collected quickly, with minimal disruption to traffic.
  • Orthoimagery, 3D models, point clouds and textured meshes.
  • Track measured and hazards flagged before they cause damage.
Monitor the vacant land around the railway track

We survey the land on both sides of the track to identify vacant railway land, boundaries and encroachments, and repeat the survey to track changes along the right-of-way.

  • Vacant parcels and encroachments identified on both sides of the track.
  • Repeat surveys track changes along the right-of-way.
  • Reports show land parcels, boundaries and encroachments for land management.

Engineering scale Planning

An engineering scale plan in railways is a detailed, technical drawing that illustrates the design and layout of railway infrastructure. This includes tracks, signals, stations, bridges, tunnels, and other related components. The purpose of such a plan is to provide a precise, scaled representation of how the railway system will be constructed and operated.

Drone survey tied to DGPS control is how we prepare ESPs. See our ESP and DPR survey page and railway yard and drainage plan case study.

Surveying and Mapping

We survey long stretches of line with photogrammetry or LiDAR, tied to DGPS ground control, to build the maps and 3D terrain models needed to plan new routes or upgrade existing ones.

Enhanced Accuracy

DGPS corrects the positional errors of standard GPS, so track alignment, station placement and other components can be designed on exact measurements.

Alignment and Layout

High-precision DGPS measurements mean new track and changes to existing track are set out accurately, with fewer costly corrections on site.

Construction Monitoring

During construction, aerial imagery and video are compared with plans and timelines, and DGPS measurements check the position of structures such as bridges and track.

Data Integration

Combining DGPS measurements and drone imagery with other data in GIS supports route planning, environmental assessment and logistics.

Deliverables

Detailed Project Reporting (DPR)

Survey, site inspection, earthwork measurement and route comparison: the base data a railway DPR needs.

DTM

A bare-earth terrain model, with buildings and vegetation removed, tied to ground control: the base for track levels, earthwork and drainage design.

Contour Mapping

Contours at the interval your design needs, for alignment, gradient and drainage design.

Orthomosaic

A continuous, geo-referenced aerial map of the corridor or yard, accurate enough to measure from and overlay on drawings.

DSM

A surface model that includes buildings, trees and structures, used to find obstructions and plan clearances along the corridor.

Aerial Photogrammetry and Videography

Aerial photos and video for planning, progress reports and documentation.

Topographical Survey

A full topographic survey of the corridor or yard: ground levels, track, structures and features in CAD.

DEM Creation

An elevation model for alignment and gradient analysis, delivered in GIS-ready formats.

Lidar Survey

LiDAR reaches the ground through gaps in vegetation, useful for corridors with tree cover or hilly terrain. See LiDAR vs photogrammetry.

Land Use Land Cover

A land use and land cover map of the corridor for land planning and environmental assessment.

Frequently asked questions

What is an Engineering Scale Plan (ESP)?

An Engineering Scale Plan is a scaled drawing of a railway station yard or section that shows tracks, points and crossings, platforms, buildings and other structures in their true positions. It is used to plan yard remodelling, new lines and other works. Drone and LiDAR surveys provide the accurate base data an ESP is drawn on.

Why use drones for railway surveys?

A drone covers a yard or a long section of track quickly without staff working on the line for long periods, which improves safety and reduces block time. Survey-grade accuracy comes from ground control and RTK or PPK positioning.

What railway deliverables can be produced?

Engineering Scale Plans, yard plans, drainage plans, longitudinal and cross sections, orthomosaics, terrain models, and GIS layers of railway land and assets.

Can metro corridors be surveyed in dense cities?

Yes, subject to flight permissions. In crowded urban areas the drone survey is planned around permitted zones and timings and is combined with ground survey where flying is restricted.
