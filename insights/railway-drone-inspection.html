Railway drone inspection: track, OHE, bridges and yards

Where drones fit in railway inspection in India: overhead equipment with thermal cameras, the track surroundings a recording car can't see, bridges under RDSO guidelines, yard plans and ESPs, and how a railway drone job is planned.

By the DATA PKT survey and inspection team · Published 23 November 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: drones do not replace track recording cars, which measure geometry and track defects. They add what those systems can't see: overhead equipment up close and in thermal (piloted by Indian Railways in the Raipur division), drains, cuttings and embankments, bridge undersides (RDSO guideline BS-129 R1), and full yard and station plans for ESPs. Most of the effort is in permissions and blocks, not in flying.

What drones are used for on Indian Railways

Indian Railways first told its zones to procure drone cameras, starting with West Central Railway, for project monitoring, the condition of track, inspections, and relief and rescue work[3][5]. West Central Railway planned them for third-line work on Bina–Katni, doubling on Katni–Singrouli, important bridge inspections and monsoon checks of deep cuttings on ghat sections[3]. A trial on the Dedicated Freight Corridor covered 98 km in three days[4].

The newer work goes further. The Ministry of Railways has reported drone-based thermal monitoring of overhead equipment piloted in the Raipur division, AI-enabled aerial inspection under development with IIT Madras, and three Integrated Track Monitoring Systems (ITMS) deployed for track inspection[1]. For bridges, RDSO issued guidelines in May 2021 for inspection using a 3D bridge model and drones (Report BS-129 R1)[2].

Overhead equipment: visual and thermal

OHE faults stop trains. A drone flown alongside the line, level with the cantilevers, shows what a ground inspection struggles to see from below or from a tower wagon at speed:

  • Contact wire and catenary: wear, kinks, broken droppers, displaced registration arms.
  • Insulators: cracks, chips and flashover marks.
  • Joints and connections: with a thermal camera, overheating clamps and jumpers stand out under load, the same principle used on power lines (see our guide to transmission line drone inspection for how hotspots are graded).
  • Masts and foundations: tilt, corrosion and damage after floods.

The Raipur pilot is exactly this use: drone thermal imaging to detect faults in OHE[1]. Flights must keep a safe distance from the 25 kV wires and run under the division's permission, with the electrical department aware of the plan.

Track, formation and drainage

Track recording cars and ITMS measure track geometry and detect defects in rails, sleepers and fastenings with machine learning and image processing[1][6]. A drone does not replace them. It adds the view around the track that a recording car cannot give:

  • Drains: blocked, silted or broken side and catch-water drains, the usual cause of formation failure in the monsoon.
  • Cuttings and embankments: cracks, slips, rock falls and erosion, especially on ghat sections[3].
  • Encroachment on railway land, and obstructions near the track.
  • Before and after: repeat flights show how a slope or drain has changed since the last monsoon. For unstable slopes, see our guide to landslide surveys.

Bridges

RDSO's BS-129 (R1) guidelines describe inspection of Indian Railway bridges using a 3D bridge information model built from drone data, and the Railway Board has asked zonal railways to integrate this "4D BrIM" approach with the Bridge Management System[2]. A drone reaches places that otherwise need scaffolding, a boat or a bridge inspection unit: the underside of girders, bearings, pier caps and the river bed around the piers. Photogrammetry turns the flight into a measurable 3D model, so cracks and spalling can be located and compared at the next inspection.

Yards, stations and project monitoring

For yards and stations, a drone survey produces the base plans that remodelling and new works start from: track centre lines, points and crossings, platforms, buildings, drains and utilities, tied to kilometre posts. That is how Engineering Scale Plans (ESPs) are prepared today; see what an ESP is. On projects such as doubling, third lines and new lines, monthly flights record progress the same way highway projects do[3].

Planning drone work on a railway

The flight is the easy part. Railway work needs permission from the division or zone that owns the section, an airspace check on Digital Sky, and coordination with the traffic and electrical departments for a block or a safe flying window. Every output is then tied to kilometre posts and chainage, so the engineering teams can use it straight away.

Frequently asked questions

Does Indian Railways use drones for inspection?

Yes. Indian Railways uses drones for project monitoring, track and infrastructure inspection, bridge inspection and relief and rescue work, and has piloted drone-based thermal monitoring of overhead equipment (OHE) in the Raipur division.

Can a drone inspect railway OHE?

Yes. A drone with a visual and a thermal camera can check the contact wire, droppers, insulators and fittings from close range and show hot joints. It must keep a safe distance from the 25 kV wires and fly under the railway's permission and safety arrangements.

Can drones replace track recording cars?

No. Track recording systems such as ITMS measure track geometry and detect defects in rails, sleepers and fastenings from the track itself. A drone complements them with a view of everything around the track: OHE, drains, cuttings, embankments, bridges and encroachments.

Is there an RDSO guideline for drone inspection of railway bridges?

Yes. RDSO Report BS-129 (R1), issued in May 2021, gives guidelines for 4D BrIM-based inspection of Indian Railway bridges using a 3D bridge information model and drones, and the Railway Board has asked zonal railways to integrate it with the Bridge Management System.

How long does a drone take to cover a railway section?

It depends on the output. For project monitoring, a drone trial on the Dedicated Freight Corridor covered 98 km in three days. Detailed OHE or bridge inspection is slower because each mast or span is flown close up.

What permissions are needed to fly a drone over a railway in India?

Permission from the zonal railway or division that owns the section, a check of the airspace on the Digital Sky platform with any permission it requires, and coordination with the traffic and electrical departments for a block or flying slot.
