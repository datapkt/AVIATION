Highway drone videography for NHAI projects: what the monthly video must show

NHAI's monthly drone video rule in plain words: who has to fly, what every frame must show, how videos reach Data Lake, how to plan a long corridor, and how the footage stands up as evidence.

By the DATA PKT survey and inspection team · Published 23 November 2026 · 10 min read

Short answer: every NHAI project, in every contract mode, must record the highway by drone every month and upload last month's and this month's videos side by side to NHAI's Data Lake portal (Policy Circular 18.69, June 2021). The contractor flies during construction, witnessed by the supervision consultant; NHAI flies before construction, at completion and in O&M. A useful video shows chainage, date and the full right of way in every frame, and follows the same flight each month.

What NHAI requires, and who it applies to

In June 2021 NHAI issued Policy Circular 18.69, Mandatory monthly drone video recording of project highway during the development, construction and O&M period. It applies to every NHAI project, whatever the mode: item rate, EPC, toll, annuity, HAM, OMT or TOT[1].

  • What to record: drone video covering the entire right of way, showing chainages, the proposed ROW and the median[1].
  • Who records it: the contractor or concessionaire, in the presence of the Team Leader of the Independent Engineer, Authority Engineer or Supervision Consultant[1].
  • How it is shown: last month's video and this month's running side by side[1], uploaded to NHAI's portal, Data Lake[2].
  • Who reviews it: supervision consultants analyse the videos and comment on them in the digital monthly progress report[3].
  • Before and after construction: NHAI's project directors carry out monthly drone surveys from signing of the contract until construction starts, at completion, and on developed projects NHAI maintains[3].
  • Which agency: NHAI has fixed zone-wise agencies and rates, but contractors may engage an agency of their choice if the specifications and methodology match NHAI's[1].

For HAM projects, reports at the time said the concessionaire must also record every six months during O&M and keep a project website with the monthly footage, and that non-compliance would attract a penalty of ₹5 lakh[4]. Check the current contract and any later circulars for the exact terms on your project.

What a good monthly video shows

A drone video that only looks good is not enough. The reviewer has to be able to stop at any frame and know where it is, when it was taken and what changed since last month. Every useful frame carries:

  1. Chainage and position on screen, so a frame can be matched to the design and to last month's video.
  2. Date and time, burnt into the video, not just in the file name.
  3. The full right of way in frame, edge to edge, with the proposed ROW visible, as the circular asks[1].
  4. The median and carriageways, so progress on each side is visible.
  5. Structures: bridges, ROBs, underpasses and culverts, recorded slowly enough to judge their stage.
  6. The same flight every month: same direction, height and speed, so the side-by-side comparison lines up.

Planning the flights on a long corridor

A national highway project can run to 50 or 100 km or more, so the monthly video is a planning job as much as a flying one:

  • Flight blocks. The corridor is split into blocks that a battery set can cover, with overlap so no chainage is missing.
  • Airspace. Each block is checked on the Digital Sky airspace map. Stretches near airports or other restricted areas need permission well before the flying date.
  • Light and weather. Flying in the same part of the day each month keeps shadows and colours comparable; rain, haze and strong wind are avoided.
  • The witness. The supervision consultant's team leader has to be present, so dates are agreed in advance[1].
  • Editing and upload. Videos are cut to chainage, overlays checked, last month's video placed beside this month's, and the files uploaded to Data Lake in the format its module asks for[5].

The video as evidence

NHAI has said the videos are stored permanently on Data Lake and can be used as evidence in dispute resolution before arbitral tribunals and courts[2]. For a contractor, that cuts both ways: the video can prove work done on time, or show that it wasn't. Three habits make it hold up:

  • Keep the unedited original files, with their metadata, alongside the uploaded edit.
  • Never re-use footage from a previous month, even for a stretch where nothing changed.
  • Make sure chainage and date are readable in every frame, not only in the title card.

Why some monthly videos don't help anyone

  • No chainage on screen, so the reviewer can't tell where a frame is.
  • A different route or height every month, so the side-by-side comparison doesn't line up.
  • Only the finished stretches are filmed, and the problem areas are skipped.
  • Too fast over structures, so the stage of a bridge or ROB can't be judged.
  • Uploaded late, missing the monthly progress report it was meant to support.

Frequently asked questions

Is drone video recording mandatory for NHAI projects?

Yes. NHAI Policy Circular 18.69 of June 2021 made monthly drone video recording mandatory for its projects in all modes, including EPC, HAM, BOT/Toll and item-rate contracts, during development, construction and operation and maintenance.

Who has to carry out the monthly drone video on an NHAI project?

During construction, the contractor or concessionaire, in the presence of the Team Leader of the Independent Engineer, Authority Engineer or Supervision Consultant. NHAI's project directors carry out monthly drone surveys from contract signing until construction starts, at completion and on projects NHAI maintains.

Where are NHAI drone videos uploaded?

To NHAI's Data Lake portal, with the current month's and last month's videos running side by side. The supervision consultant reviews them and comments in the digital monthly progress report.

Can a contractor use any drone agency for NHAI videos?

Yes. The circular says NHAI has empanelled agencies zone-wise at fixed rates, but contractors and concessionaires may engage an agency of their choice as long as the specifications and methodology match those notified by NHAI.

Can NHAI drone videos be used as evidence?

NHAI has said the videos are stored permanently on Data Lake and can be used as evidence in dispute resolution before arbitral tribunals and courts. That makes clear chainage, date and location on every frame important.

How many kilometres of highway can a drone team record in a day?

Our teams plan about 50 km of videography a day, or about 35 km of survey imagery, in good weather on open corridors. Airspace permissions, weather and the length of each flight block change the daily figure.
