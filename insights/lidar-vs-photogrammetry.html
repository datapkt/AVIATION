LiDAR vs photogrammetry: which drone survey do you need?

LiDAR or photogrammetry? How each works, accuracy, vegetation, cost and deliverables compared, with the right choice for highways, railways, power lines, mines and hills.

By the DATA PKT survey and GIS engineering team · Published 27 October 2026

Short answer: choose photogrammetry for open ground when you want a true-colour map at the lowest cost. Choose LiDAR when the ground is under trees, when you need power lines or thin structures in 3D, or when the terrain is steep and forested. Long corridors often use both.

How each one works

Photogrammetry builds a map and 3D model from hundreds of overlapping photos. Software matches the same points across photos to work out their positions. LiDAR fires laser pulses from the drone and times their return. Some pulses pass through gaps in leaves and reach the ground, which is why LiDAR can map terrain under a canopy.

Frequently asked questions

Is LiDAR more accurate than photogrammetry?

On open, hard ground with good control, both reach a few centimetres. LiDAR's advantage is under vegetation and on thin objects like wires, where photogrammetry cannot see the ground or reconstruct the object.

Which is cheaper, LiDAR or photogrammetry?

Photogrammetry is usually more economical, because the sensor is cheaper and the drones are smaller. LiDAR earns its cost where the ground is hidden by trees or the project needs wires and fine 3D detail.

Can I get a colour map from LiDAR?

Most survey LiDAR systems carry a camera as well, so the point cloud can be coloured and an orthomosaic produced from the same flight.
