Engineering Scale Plans (ESP) and DPR Surveys

Drone surveys are now widely used in railway engineering for planning, monitoring and maintenance. Here is how our drone data supports engineering scale planning on railways:

Surveying and Mapping

Our drones carry high-resolution cameras and LiDAR sensors to survey long stretches of railway line quickly and accurately. The data produces detailed maps and 3D terrain models for planning new routes or upgrading existing ones.

Inspection and Maintenance

Our drones inspect hard-to-reach structures such as bridges, tunnels and elevated tracks without costly, slow manual access. High-resolution and thermal imagery can show structural defects, track alignment problems and vegetation encroachment.

Monitoring Construction Progress

Drone surveys reduce the time staff spend in hazardous locations. We can also survey areas hit by landslides or floods to provide data for risk assessment and mitigation.

Asset Management

Accurate, current drone data helps railways manage track, stations and other assets, supporting planned maintenance and reducing service disruptions.

Environmental Assessment

Environmental assessment matters on railway projects, especially where lines are extended through sensitive areas. We monitor environmental impact, track change over time and support compliance with environmental regulations.

Disaster Response

After an accident or natural disaster, we can quickly survey the damage to support emergency response, decision-making and recovery work.

Cost and Time Efficiency

Drone surveys take less time and cost less than traditional survey and inspection methods. We cover large areas quickly, and our in-house processing shortens delivery.

Precision and Accuracy

Drone data tied to DGPS ground control points gives the accuracy that engineering design and approvals depend on.

Data Integration and Analysis

Our drone data is delivered in GeoTIFF, LAS, DWG and SHP formats for analysis, planning and visualisation in Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Building Information Modelling (BIM).

Our drone data gives railway engineering scale planning accurate, detailed and current information, improving the efficiency, safety and cost of railway works.

What an Engineering Scale Plan shows

An Engineering Scale Plan (ESP) is the railway's master drawing of a station yard or section, drawn to scale with every feature in its true position. Engineering, signalling, traction and operating departments all work from it, and it is the base on which yard remodelling, new lines and other works are planned, checked and approved. An ESP prepared from a drone survey typically shows:

  • Track: running lines, loops and sidings, track centres, and the kilometrage along the section
  • Points and crossings: every turnout with its position and direction
  • Platforms and passenger structures: platform lengths and edges, foot-over-bridges, ramps, shelters and station buildings
  • Crossings of the line: level crossings, road over-bridges and road under-bridges
  • Traction and signalling: OHE masts and portals, signals and cabins
  • Bridges, culverts and drains, with their position on the section
  • Buildings and land: goods sheds, service buildings, quarters, boundary walls and fencing, and the railway land boundary
  • Levels: spot levels and contours for drainage, formation and earthwork

How we prepare an ESP

  1. Scope and permissions: the yard limits, the chainage and the drawing standard are fixed with the railway, and flights are cleared with the station and the airspace authority.
  2. DGPS control: ground control points and checkpoints are set around the yard and tied to the railway's reference benchmarks.
  3. Drone flights: the yard is captured with overlapping images, and with LiDAR where trees or structures hide the ground.
  4. Processing: the images become an orthomosaic, a surface model and a terrain model with contours.
  5. Ground survey: rail levels, platform heights and details the drone cannot see are measured with DGPS and total station.
  6. Drafting: tracks, turnouts, structures and features are drawn in CAD, in the layers and scale the railway specifies.
  7. Checking and submission: the drawing is verified on site with railway staff, corrected and submitted, with revisions until it is accepted.

DPR surveys

A Detailed Project Report is only as good as the survey under it. For railway and road DPRs we provide the survey and mapping inputs the consultant and the authority need to fix the alignment, the structures and the cost:

  • Topographic strip survey of the corridor, from drone photogrammetry or LiDAR with DGPS control
  • Alignment options compared on the orthomosaic and terrain model
  • Longitudinal and cross-sections for earthwork and formation design
  • Bridges and drainage: catchments from the terrain model, waterway sections and existing structures
  • Land: the corridor laid over khasra (revenue) maps, showing the land and owners affected
  • Utilities and obstructions: power lines, pipelines, buildings and other features to be shifted or avoided
  • Quantities: earthwork and other quantities measured from the survey data

Why drones for railway yards

  • Safety: the yard is captured from the air, so the survey team spends far less time on or beside running lines.
  • Speed: one drone team captures around 5 sq km of survey data in a day, so a yard is flown in hours rather than surveyed over weeks.
  • Little disturbance to traffic: flights do not need the line, and the ground work left is short and planned with the station.
  • A complete record: the orthomosaic and terrain model stay on file, so later questions can be answered without returning to site.

Accuracy comes from the DGPS ground control and is proved on independent checkpoints. Read more in how accurate a drone survey is and what an Engineering Scale Plan is.

Frequently asked questions

What is an Engineering Scale Plan (ESP)?

An Engineering Scale Plan is a scaled drawing of a railway station yard or section showing tracks, points and crossings, platforms, buildings and other features in their true positions. Railways use ESPs to plan and approve yard remodelling, new lines and other works.

How does a drone survey help prepare an ESP?

A drone survey with ground control captures the whole yard as an accurate orthomosaic and terrain model in a short time. Drafters then trace tracks, structures and levels from this base, and the field team checks critical details on the ground.

What survey inputs does a DPR need?

A Detailed Project Report usually needs topography of the corridor, existing features and utilities, levels for drainage and earthwork, and land details. Drone, LiDAR and DGPS surveys provide these as drawings and GIS layers.

Does DATA PKT design the project itself?

We provide survey, mapping and design inputs such as drawings and plans. We do not construct projects.

Which railway authorities has DATA PKT worked with?

Our railway clients include Central Railway, North Central Railway, Eastern Railway, Bridge & Roof Co. (India) Ltd and IRCON International, among other railway and metro organisations.

What does an Engineering Scale Plan show?

Tracks and track centres, points and crossings, platforms, foot-over-bridges, level crossings, station buildings and other structures, OHE masts, signals, bridges and culverts, drains, the railway boundary, kilometrage and levels, all in their true positions.

Do you need a traffic block to survey a station yard?

Usually not for the drone flights, which capture the yard from the air. Ground checks of rail levels and details are planned with the station and engineering staff, and kept short.

How accurate is a drone survey for an ESP?

The survey is tied to DGPS ground control points and checked against independent checkpoints, and critical details such as rail levels are measured on the ground. We report the accuracy achieved on the checkpoints with every survey.

In what format are ESPs delivered?

As CAD drawings in the layers and scale the railway asks for, with PDF prints, the orthomosaic, the terrain model and contours, and GIS files on request.
