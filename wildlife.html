Forest mapping

Drones fitted with cameras and other sensors collect high-resolution imagery for detailed forest maps. These maps are used to track changes over time, identify deforestation or illegal logging, and study tree health and growth.

Topographic survey & boundary definition

Drone topographical surveys map land contours, elevations and terrain features quickly and precisely. High-resolution imagery and LiDAR data support accurate boundary demarcation. Compared with conventional surveying, this saves time and resources, and the data supports land development, infrastructure planning and environmental management.

Forest health mapping

Drones with high-resolution and thermal cameras can examine individual trees for signs of disease or damage, so forest managers can act before problems spread. The data helps plan measures to maintain tree health and protect forest ecosystems.

Wildlife Monitoring

Drones let researchers observe animal movement and behaviour in forests without disturbing them, giving useful information on the health of many species. From above, researchers can track animals across a wide area.

This is especially useful for animals that are hard to track on foot. Cameras and other sensors on the drone record behaviour, count individuals and detect movement patterns.

  • Drones provide a non-intrusive method for observing elusive or nocturnal species, such as owls or bats, without disturbing their natural habitats.
  • By capturing high-resolution imagery and video footage, drones enable detailed analysis of animal interactions, feeding behaviors, and habitat preferences.
  • Live video lets researchers adjust their observations during the flight.
  • Thermal imaging sensors help detect animals hidden under dense foliage or in low light.
Fire Detection and Management

Drones coupled with thermal imaging cameras provide rapid response and important insights into forest fire detection and management. They can track the spread of fires, assess damage, and offer real-time data to ground-based firefighters. Drones help suppress fire outbreaks and reduce their impact on forest ecosystems.

  • Detect forest fires early and track their spread with thermal imaging drones, so crews can intervene sooner.
  • Give ground-based firefighters live data on fire behaviour, intensity and direction to coordinate their response.
  • Assess the extent of fire damage and identify areas of concern within the forest ecosystem, guiding post-fire recovery and restoration efforts.
  • Identify hotspots and direct firefighting resources to the areas that need them most.
  • Monitor fire behaviour to help protect sensitive habitats and biodiversity.

Frequently asked questions

How are drones used for wildlife monitoring?

Drones let researchers observe animal movement and behaviour across a wide area without disturbing them. Cameras and other sensors record behaviour, count individuals and show movement patterns, live video lets observers adjust during the flight, and thermal sensors help detect animals hidden under dense foliage or in low light.

What forest mapping services do you offer?

We carry out drone topographical surveys and monitoring of forests, mangroves and wetlands, covering tree counting, vegetation health mapping, encroachment analysis, biomass estimation, forest carbon stock mapping, biodiversity mapping and wildlife corridor mapping. Repeat maps track change over time and help identify deforestation or illegal logging.

Can drones help detect and manage forest fires?

Yes. Thermal imaging drones help detect fires early, track their spread and identify hotspots, and give ground-based firefighters live data on fire behaviour, intensity and direction. After a fire, drone imagery helps assess the extent of damage and guide recovery and restoration work.

Can you survey mangroves and wetlands by drone?

Yes. We choose sensors to suit each environment, from natural and urban forests to mangroves and wetlands. High-resolution imagery and LiDAR data support topographic mapping and accurate boundary demarcation, which is useful where these areas are hard to cover on foot.
