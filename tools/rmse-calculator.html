Using the calculator

Enter one checkpoint per row, either as surveyed and map coordinates or as residuals you have already worked out. Use projected coordinates in metres or feet, such as UTM, not latitude and longitude, and make sure both sets are in the same coordinate system and height datum. The unit setting only labels the results; it does not convert your numbers. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

What RMSE measures

RMSE (root mean square error) is the usual single figure for the accuracy of a map, a digital terrain model or a point cloud. For each checkpoint the residual is the value read from the map minus the surveyed value. Square the residuals, take their mean and then the square root:

RMSEx = √( Σ dX² ÷ n ) · RMSEy = √( Σ dY² ÷ n ) · RMSEz = √( Σ dZ² ÷ n )

Squaring stops positive and negative errors cancelling out, and it gives large errors more weight than small ones, so one bad point shows up clearly. See RMSE in the glossary.

Horizontal and vertical accuracy

Horizontal accuracy combines the X and Y errors into one radial figure:

RMSEr = √( RMSEx² + RMSEy² )

Vertical accuracy, RMSEz, is reported on its own because heights behave differently. On a drone photogrammetry survey the vertical error is usually larger than the horizontal error, and it also depends on the ground cover: bare, hard surfaces test better than grass or crops. The GSD calculator gives a rule-of-thumb link between pixel size and the accuracy you can expect.

Why checkpoints must be held out

Checkpoints are surveyed exactly like ground control points (GCPs) but are not used to process the map. If a point helped to fix the model, the model will fit it closely whatever the real error is, so testing against GCPs makes a survey look better than it is. Use independent checkpoints, spread them over the whole site or along the whole corridor, place them on firm, clearly visible ground, and measure them in the same coordinate system and height datum as the deliverable. Our guide on how accurate a drone survey is explains how ground control and checkpoints are laid out.

How to read the result

  • Compare RMSE with the specification. The contract or the standard you work to sets the target, for example a horizontal and a vertical RMSE in centimetres. Check that both figures meet it.
  • Look at the mean error. If the mean error on an axis is close to the RMSE, most of the error is a shift in one direction. That usually points to a datum, geoid or control problem rather than random noise, and it can often be corrected.
  • Check the largest residual. One point far worse than the rest may be a mis-identified target, a disturbed marker or a typing error. Investigate it before you remove it, and report any point you leave out.
  • Count the points. The 2023 ASPRS standard asks for at least 30 checkpoints for a product accuracy assessment. With fewer, the RMSE is still a useful check but a less reliable estimate.

For contour and terrain work, the checkpoint test is what proves the DTM. Read more in topographic and contour survey by drone.

Frequently asked questions

What is RMSE in a drone survey?

RMSE, or root mean square error, is the square root of the average of the squared differences between map coordinates and surveyed checkpoint coordinates. It is calculated separately for X, Y and Z and is the usual single figure for a survey's accuracy.

How is horizontal RMSE calculated?

Horizontal RMSE, often written RMSEr, combines the two horizontal axes: RMSEr = √(RMSEx² + RMSEy²). Vertical accuracy is reported separately as RMSEz.

How many checkpoints do I need?

The 2023 ASPRS standard asks for at least 30 checkpoints for a product accuracy assessment, spread across the whole area. With fewer checkpoints the RMSE is still a useful check, but it is a less reliable estimate of the true accuracy.

What does the mean error tell me?

The mean error, or bias, shows whether the errors lean in one direction. If it is close to the RMSE on an axis, most of the error is a systematic shift, which usually points to a datum, geoid or ground control problem rather than random noise.
