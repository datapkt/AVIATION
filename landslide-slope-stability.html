Landslide and slope stability investigation and protection design

For hill roads, railways and project sites, DATA PKT finds which slopes are likely to fail, why, and what will hold them: drone LiDAR and 3D models, seismic refraction, geological mapping, stability analysis and the design of slope protection.

What we investigateRoad and rail cut slopes, embankments, hill sites and rockfall zones
MethodsDrone LiDAR and 3D models, seismic refraction tomography, geological mapping, DGPS
What you getFactor of safety per section, hazard map, priority list, protection design
Recent project~66 km road corridor in Assam

In short: DATA PKT is a landslide and slope stability investigation company. We combine three independent data sets in one ground model: drone LiDAR for the shape of the slope, seismic refraction tomography for what lies below the surface, and geological mapping for how the soil and rock behave. A stability analysis turns that model into a factor of safety for every critical section, and we design the protection each stretch needs.

Why slopes fail, and why one method is not enough

Most slope failures along Indian hill roads and railways come from a few mechanisms: shallow slides in soil and weathered rock after heavy rain, erosion and scour at the toe, and rock blocks sliding or toppling along joints. Each needs different information. A terrain model shows the geometry but not what is underneath; a borehole shows one point; a geologist sees the rock face but not its depth. That is why we combine the methods below and check them against each other.

What we do

1. Drone LiDAR survey and 3D models

RTK/PPK drone LiDAR tied to DGPS ground control captures the slope and the road edge, even under vegetation. Multiple laser returns let the point cloud be classified into ground and vegetation, so we deliver a bare-earth digital terrain model (DTM), a surface model (DSM), contours, an orthophoto, slope-angle and aspect maps, longitudinal and cross-sections, and 3D models of critical stretches. See LiDAR survey.

2. Seismic refraction tomography (SRT)

A line of geophones (typically 24 channels) is laid on the slope or road edge and a hammer source is struck at several shot points along it. First-arrival times are picked and inverted into a P-wave velocity section that shows the thickness of loose cover, the depth to weathered and fresh rock, and low-velocity zones that are weak or saturated. Every profile is positioned by DGPS so it plots directly on the LiDAR cross-sections.

3. Engineering geological mapping

Geologists map lithology and structure at the critical locations, measure joints by scanline and window surveys, test strength in the field with a Schmidt hammer and point-load tester, and classify the rock mass with RMR, GSI and SMR. Stereonets and kinematic analysis show which failure modes are possible: planar sliding, wedge sliding or toppling.

4. Strength parameters

Shear strength (cohesion c′ and friction angle φ′) is derived from GSI and field strength through the generalised Hoek–Brown criterion, with Barton–Bandis for joints, cross-checked against the SRT velocities. Laboratory tests on borehole samples are recommended for the most critical sections.

5. Slope stability analysis

Limit-equilibrium analysis (Bishop, Spencer and Morgenstern–Price methods) gives a factor of safety for each critical section in three cases: dry, saturated (monsoon) and earthquake (pseudo-static, with the seismic coefficient for the site's IS 1893 zone). Rock slopes are also checked for planar and wedge failure.

6. Hazard zonation and protection design

Every stretch is ranked Low, Medium, High or Critical, so the owner knows where to act first. For each stretch we recommend and design protection matched to its failure mechanism:

Standards and practice

DGPS control tied to Survey of India or CORS; LiDAR accuracy reported against independent check points (ASPRS); discontinuity surveys to IS 11315; point-load testing to IS 8764; RMR to IS 13365; seismic zones and coefficients to IS 1893; roadside work-zone safety to IRC:SP:55; drone operations under the Drone Rules, 2021 with Digital Sky permissions.

Where this matters

Hill roads and railways in Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram and Sikkim, the Himalayan states, the Western Ghats and the Nilgiris; tunnel portals and cuttings; hydropower, transmission and pipeline corridors through hills. See our work in Northeast India.

Frequently asked questions

What does a slope stability investigation include?

Three independent data sets combined in one ground model: drone LiDAR for the shape of the slope, seismic refraction tomography for what lies below the surface, and geological mapping for how the soil and rock behave. A stability analysis then gives a factor of safety for each critical section, and the slopes are ranked by hazard with remedial measures.

What is seismic refraction tomography (SRT) used for on slopes?

SRT sends sound waves into the ground from a hammer source and records them on a line of geophones. The speed of the waves shows the thickness of loose soil, the depth to weathered and fresh rock, and weak or saturated zones, which sets the length of soil nails and anchors and the depth of possible slip surfaces.

Why use drone LiDAR instead of a photo survey for slopes?

Slopes beside hill roads are usually under trees and shrubs. LiDAR records several returns from each laser pulse, so the ground under the vegetation can be classified and a bare-earth terrain model produced. That gives true slope angles, heights, scarps and cross-sections where a photo survey would only see the canopy.

What are RMR, GSI and SMR?

They are rock mass classification systems. RMR (Rock Mass Rating) and GSI (Geological Strength Index) describe rock quality from strength, joints and groundwater; SMR (Slope Mass Rating) adjusts RMR for how the joints sit relative to the slope face. Together with field strength tests they give the strength parameters used in the stability analysis.

Does DATA PKT design slope protection?

Yes. From the investigation we prepare the design inputs and the design of protection measures such as drainage, soil nails and anchors, flexible high-tensile mesh, rockfall drapes and barriers, and erosion control, matched to each stretch's failure mechanism and hazard level. We survey, investigate and design; we do not install.

Which areas of India need this most?

Hill roads and railways in the Himalaya, the Northeast, the Western Ghats and the Nilgiris, where heavy monsoon rain, weak weathered rock and seismic activity combine. Much of the Northeast lies in seismic Zone V under IS 1893, the highest zone.
