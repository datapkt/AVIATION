Upstream

Upstream work includes site selection, surveys, drilling, exploration and production of crude oil and gas. Drones make this traditionally slow, manual process faster and more accurate in the following ways:

Provide up-to-date data at every stage.

  • Thermal sensors and cameras on drones provide data for identifying suitable transfer routes and related infrastructure.
  • During construction, managers can remotely supervise progress using real-time video feeds from drones, facilitating timely decision-making and project oversight.
  • Designers can check work in progress against the plans and see where changes are needed.
  • Drones support predictive maintenance by regularly collecting data on issues such as leaks and corrosion, so maintenance can be planned before failures occur.
  • Data relayed from drones in real time shortens the gap between collection and action, so problems can be dealt with promptly.

Midstream

Midstream is the transport of crude oil and gas from upstream sites to downstream processing and storage, by pipeline, barge or tanker. Pipelines may run long distances above ground or underwater, and the product often needs secure storage during treatment and before onward transport.

Secure storage is needed throughout midstream operations, to deal with transport delays and surplus stock, and to hold product between treatment and onward transit.

  • Infrastructure for midstream operations also encompasses storage facilities such as tanks, terminals, and depots, strategically located along transportation routes to facilitate smooth transitions between different modes of transportation and accommodate fluctuations in demand.
  • Midstream operations are critical to a reliable energy supply and are subject to strict safety and environmental regulations covering the transport and storage of crude oil and gas.

Downstream

Standing at the conclusion of the cycle, downstream serves as a link between oil and gas businesses and end consumers (B2B, B2C & B2G). At this stage, crude oil and raw natural gas are refined to produce end products such as gasoline, jet fuel, heating oil, natural gas, LPG, and other petrochemicals.

Because downstream is closest to the end user, considerable people and resources go into delivering products on time and with minimal environmental impact. Industrial waste must also be disposed of safely.

Drones help in midstream and downstream operations in the following ways:

Pipeline Inspections

The inspection of pipelines is one of the most important applications of drones in the oil and gas industry. Drones provide an aerial perspective that allows for comprehensive analysis of pipeline conditions without having to shut down the pipeline. A pipeline inspection drone can spot environmental encroachments, physical damage, corrosion, leaks, and spills.

Gas Flare Stack Inspections

Gas flare stacks are used to burn off waste gases from oil and gas production. Flare stack inspections are critical to maintaining safety and compliance. A drone can capture high-resolution images and thermal imaging to detect leaks, gas combustion, and other potential safety hazards. Through drone inspections, maintenance checks can be done without having to shut down production.

Power Line Inspections

Power lines are critical to the oil and gas industry. Drones can provide effective visual inspections and thermographic imaging to detect damage, wear and tear, and hot spots on power lines. Inspections can be done much more efficiently than traditional methods, reducing costs and minimising safety risks.

Oil Rig Inspections

Drones can inspect oil rig platforms, derricks, and equipment. With infrared imaging, drones can detect potential equipment failures and cracks that might not be visible from the ground. A drone inspection of drilling platforms increases cost-effectiveness, efficacy, and safety.

Tank Inspections

Tanks are crucial elements in the oil and gas industry. Routine inspections are necessary to detect leaks, surface corrosion, or other damage to the tank. Drones can detect surface level changes, leaks, and other issues that might not be visible to the naked eye. A drone inspection of tanks reduces safety risks and maintenance costs.

Frequently asked questions

What drone services do you provide for the oil and gas industry?

We provide thermal and visual inspection of plants and pipelines, pipeline surveillance, aerial videography for change detection, aerial topographical surveys for greenfield projects, construction progress monitoring and sensor-based leakage and risk investigations, supporting upstream, midstream and downstream operations.

Can a pipeline be inspected by drone without shutting it down?

Yes. A drone inspects the pipeline from the air while it stays in service. The imagery can show environmental encroachment, physical damage, corrosion, leaks and spills, and regular flights build a record that supports planned maintenance before failures occur.

How is a gas flare stack inspected by drone?

A drone captures high-resolution and thermal images of the flare stack to detect leaks, check gas combustion and spot other safety hazards. Because the drone does the close-up work, these maintenance checks can be done without shutting down production.

Can drones inspect storage tanks and oil rigs?

Yes. Drones can inspect tanks for leaks, surface corrosion, surface level changes and other damage that may not be visible to the eye, and infrared imaging of rig platforms, derricks and equipment can reveal cracks and early signs of equipment failure, without sending staff into hazardous positions.

Is your thermal inspection team certified?

Our thermal team includes a thermographer certified to ISO 9712 Level 2, and our plant and pipeline inspections combine thermal and visual imagery. ISO 9712 is the international standard for qualifying and certifying non-destructive testing personnel, a scheme that includes infrared thermography.
