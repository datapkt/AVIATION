Case study · Water and irrigation

Groundwater recharge system for Mahendragarh: survey and design of two canal-fed lines

In Mahendragarh district, in southern Haryana, DATA PKT is surveying and designing a groundwater recharge system. Two lines, of about 45 km and 15 km, take water from an existing canal or river and carry it to structures that return it to the aquifer.

LocationMahendragarh (Mahindergarh) district, Haryana
PurposeRecharge groundwater with surplus canal or river water
NetworkTwo lines, about 45 km and 15 km long
Our scopeDrone and DGPS survey, planning and design of the recharge system

In short: water that a canal or river carries in surplus is drawn off, carried along two lines and put back into the ground through recharge structures. DATA PKT surveys the corridors with drones and DGPS and designs the system on that survey: the off-take, the alignment and levels of each line, the crossings and the recharge structures.

The need

Mahendragarh is a semi-arid district in southern Haryana. Farms and villages draw most of their water from the ground, rainfall is short and seasonal, and groundwater levels have been falling for years. Canals and rivers carry more water at some times of the year than is used. A recharge system stores that surplus underground, where it does not evaporate and can be pumped back by wells across the area.

How the recharge system works

The system is designed as a chain, from the source to the aquifer. Each link is sized from the survey levels and the water that can be drawn:

  1. Off-take at the canal or river: an intake with a regulator or gate, so water is drawn only when it is available and in the quantity allowed.
  2. Silt control: a desilting or sedimentation chamber and filter, because silt that reaches a recharge well clogs it.
  3. Conveyance lines: the two lines, about 45 km and 15 km, laid to an alignment and gradient that move water by gravity wherever the ground allows.
  4. Crossings: structures where the lines cross roads, railways, other canals, drains and utilities.
  5. Recharge structures: recharge wells and shafts, percolation pits and recharge ponds, and existing village ponds revived to hold and soak water, placed where the ground can take it.
  6. Escapes and monitoring: outlets for excess water, and points to watch water levels so the effect can be measured.

How drones make the design possible

A recharge network is a long, linear project across farmland and villages. Designing it needs the levels and features of every kilometre, and a conventional ground survey of 60 km would take weeks. A drone survey tied to DGPS ground control captures the corridors in days and gives the designers the whole picture at once.

Why drones are faster

A conventional ground survey with DGPS and total station measures one point at a time, so a team covers a small area each day and a long corridor takes weeks. A DATA PKT drone team captures around 5 sq km of survey data in a day, which is then mapped and analysed in the office. On a linear corridor such as a highway, one team captures about 35 km of drone imagery for photogrammetry, or about 50 km of aerial video, in a day, and DATA PKT has more than 20 drone pilots (a team of about 22) to put in the field. Ground instruments are still used, but only where they matter most: for the control points that fix the map to real coordinates, for checkpoints that prove the accuracy, and for detail the drone cannot see.

How we run the survey and design

  1. Desk study: canal and river records, existing maps and groundwater information for the area.
  2. Reconnaissance: the off-take point, the possible routes and the recharge sites are walked and photographed.
  3. DGPS control: ground control points and benchmarks are set along both corridors, so every map and level is tied to real coordinates.
  4. Drone mapping: the 45 km and 15 km corridors are flown and processed into orthomosaics, terrain models and contours.
  5. Ground survey: levels at the canal or river, at crossings and at structure sites are checked with DGPS and total station.
  6. Design: alignment, longitudinal sections, hydraulic sizing of the lines, and drawings of the off-take, silt chambers, crossings and recharge structures.
  7. Hand-over: drawings, sections, quantities and the survey data, in CAD and GIS formats.

Other irrigation, canal and river surveys

Frequently asked questions

What is a groundwater recharge system?

A network that takes surplus water from a canal or river when it is available and lets it soak back into the aquifer through recharge structures such as recharge wells and shafts, percolation pits and ponds, instead of letting it run off or evaporate.

What is DATA PKT doing in Mahendragarh?

We are carrying out the survey and design of a groundwater recharge system for the Mahendragarh area in Haryana. Two lines, of about 45 km and 15 km, take water from an existing canal or river and carry it to the recharge structures.

How do drones help design a recharge system?

A drone survey tied to DGPS control maps the whole corridor as an orthomosaic and terrain model in days. Designers use it to choose the alignment, check that water can flow by gravity, draw longitudinal and cross-sections, find crossings and pick recharge sites such as low ground and village ponds.

How long does it take to survey a 45 km line?

One DATA PKT drone team captures about 35 km of linear corridor imagery in a day, so the flying for both lines takes a few days. DGPS control, ground checks and processing come on top.

Does DATA PKT survey canals and rivers for irrigation departments?

Yes. We survey canals, distributaries, minors, drains and rivers: longitudinal and cross-sections, bathymetry of the water channel, structures such as regulators, falls and bridges, encroachments, lining condition and command areas.
