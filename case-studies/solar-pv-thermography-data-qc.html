Solar PV thermography in Tamil Nadu: what data QC catches before the report

At a utility-scale solar plant of about 340 MWp in Tamil Nadu, DATA PKT set the scope of the drone thermal and RGB acquisition and checked every block of data before analysis. This is what that check found, and what we now put in every solar flight brief.

Published 25 November 2026

LocationTamil Nadu
PlantAbout 340 MWp; 19 blocks; about 1.03 million modules
ScopeDrone thermal (radiometric IR) and RGB acquisition, processing and QC
StatusRe-flight of affected areas agreed; report follows on data that passes QC

In short: a partner flight crew flew a solar PV plant of about 340 MWp in Tamil Nadu in mid-October 2026, working to DATA PKT's scope. Before any thermal analysis, DATA PKT's processing QC checked the data against the IEC TS 62446-3 capture conditions, overlap and positioning. It found blocks split across several days, image positions off by several metres because there was no RTK or ground control, and edge tables not covered by two flight lines. DATA PKT identified the areas to be recaptured, and the partner agreed to re-fly them. The thermography report is prepared only from data that passes these checks.

Scope and plant

The work was drone-based thermal (radiometric IR) and RGB data acquisition, with processing and quality control by DATA PKT. The flights were made by a partner flight crew working to DATA PKT's scope and to the plant layout (KML) that DATA PKT supplied.

Plant capacityAbout 340 MWp, utility-scale solar PV

Modules1,025,976 (about 1.03 million), rated 330–340 Wp

How the plant was flown

The partner crew flew the plant between 12 and 18 October 2026 to DATA PKT's scope, using the plant layout KML to define each block. Intermittent rain forced pauses, so some blocks were flown over several days instead of in one session.

The thermal capture rule

Thermal images only show module faults reliably when the modules are working under enough sunlight and conditions are stable. IEC TS 62446-3, the technical specification for aerial thermography of PV plants, sets a minimum irradiance of 600 W/m² in the plane of the modules. On this plant the rule was:

  • IR imagery captured only before 16:00, and only with plane-of-array irradiance at or above 600 W/m².
  • Flights after 16:00 captured RGB only.

For what each capture condition means in practice, see our guide to drone versus manual solar panel inspection.

What processing QC found

DATA PKT checks every acquisition against the IEC TS 62446-3 conditions, overlap and positioning before analysis starts. On this plant the checks found three problems.

CheckWhat QC foundWhy it matters for the report
One session per blockSeveral blocks split across days: one block over four days, nine blocks over three daysIrradiance, temperature and wind change from day to day, so modules in the same block can be imaged under different conditions, which makes temperature differences within the block harder to compare
PositioningNo RTK and no ground control, so image positions were off by several metresThe report locates each anomaly by block, string and module. A position error of several metres can place an anomaly on the wrong table or string
Overlap and coverageSide overlap of about 65 % (flight lines 13.5 m apart), with lines stopping at the last table row, so some edge tables were not covered by two linesModules at block edges are seen in fewer images, so they are harder to map and check than modules in the middle of a block

What DATA PKT did

  1. Requested the flight records and mission files from the partner: the DJI Pilot 2 FlightRecords and the KMZ mission files.
  2. Identified the areas to be recaptured.
  3. Passed the recapture areas to the partner, who agreed to re-fly them.

Why QC comes before analysis

A solar thermography report is a list of anomalies, each with its type, location (block, string, module), temperature difference and severity. Every one of those fields depends on the acquisition. If images were captured outside valid conditions, if a block mixes days, if positions are off, or if edge tables are missing from the overlap, the report inherits the problem.

Checking the data first means the problem is fixed in the data, by recapture, rather than explained in the report. It also means the O&M team can trust that an anomaly marked on a table is on that table.

What DATA PKT checks on every acquisition

  • Capture conditions: that thermal images fall inside the IEC TS 62446-3 conditions, with plane-of-array irradiance at or above 600 W/m², and inside the capture window set for the site.
  • Sessions: which days each block was flown on.
  • Overlap and coverage: line spacing, side overlap, and whether edge tables are covered by two lines.
  • Positioning: whether the images are tied to RTK, PPK or ground control, and how well they fit the plant layout.

What we now specify in every solar flight brief

These are DATA PKT's requirements going forward, for our own crews and for partner crews flying to our survey plan:

  • Positioning: RTK or PPK on the drone, or ground control points, so that anomalies land on the right table, string and module. See how GSD, GCPs and RTK set drone survey accuracy.
  • Higher side overlap than was flown on this plant.
  • One session per block: each block completed in one session where weather allows.
  • Flight lines extended past the last table row, so edge tables are covered by two lines.
  • Thermal capture only in valid conditions: IEC TS 62446-3 conditions, with plane-of-array irradiance at or above 600 W/m², and RGB only outside the thermal window.
  • Flight logs and mission files delivered with the data, so that every block can be traced to how it was flown.

Where the project stands

The partner has agreed to re-fly the areas DATA PKT identified. The thermography analysis and report are prepared only from data that passes the same checks.

Frequently asked questions

What did this project cover?

Drone-based thermal (radiometric IR) and RGB data acquisition at a utility-scale solar PV plant of about 340 MWp in Tamil Nadu, with processing and quality control by DATA PKT. The plant has 19 blocks, 16,548 module structures, 33,096 strings and about 1.03 million modules rated 330–340 Wp.

What conditions must thermal images of a solar plant meet?

IEC TS 62446-3 sets a minimum irradiance of 600 W/m² in the plane of the modules, with stable conditions. On this plant, IR imagery was captured only before 16:00 and only with plane-of-array irradiance at or above 600 W/m². Flights after 16:00 were RGB only.

Why do RTK or ground control matter for a thermography report?

The report locates each anomaly by block, string and module. Without RTK or ground control, image positions on this plant were off by several metres, which can place an anomaly on the wrong table or string. DATA PKT now specifies RTK or PPK, or ground control points, in every solar flight brief.

Is the thermography report complete?

No. DATA PKT's QC identified the areas to be recaptured, and the partner flight crew agreed to re-fly them. The analysis and report are prepared only from data that passes the same checks.
