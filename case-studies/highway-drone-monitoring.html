Highway drone monitoring: more than 3 lakh km of data for authorities and contractors

For highway authorities, highway contractors and private developers, DATA PKT sets DGPS control, flies drone photogrammetry and aerial videography every month, and turns the data into analytics that show progress and problems on the road.

Published 26 October 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

ClientsHighway authorities, highway contractors and private developers
Services usedDrone photogrammetry, aerial videography, DGPS control and analytics
Highway data capturedMore than 3 lakh km in six years, imagery and videography
EmpanelmentThree NHAI zones

In short: every month our pilots fly each highway package along the same path, tied to DGPS ground control. The imagery becomes georeferenced maps (photogrammetry), the video becomes the comparative monthly record, and both feed analytics that measure the rate of completion and flag anomalies on completed highways.

What a drone service provider does

Highway drone programmes separate two roles. The drone service provider (DSP) works in the field and on the data: it flies the highway, captures it and turns it into information. The technology service provider (TSP) runs the technology platform the data goes into. As a DSP, DATA PKT delivers:

  • DGPS ground control, so every map is tied to real coordinates
  • Drone photogrammetry: georeferenced orthomosaics and measurements of each package
  • Aerial videography: the comparative monthly video
  • Analytics on that data, including AI analytics, described below

Why drones are faster

A ground survey with DGPS and total station measures one point at a time, so a long corridor takes weeks. A DATA PKT drone team captures around 5 sq km of survey data in a day, which is then mapped and analysed in the office.

On a linear corridor such as a highway, one team captures about 35 km of drone imagery for photogrammetry, or about 50 km of aerial video, in a day, and DATA PKT has more than 20 drone pilots (a team of about 22) to put in the field. Ground instruments are still used, but only where they matter most: for the control points that fix the map to real coordinates, for checkpoints that prove the accuracy, and for detail the drone cannot see.

Why highways need a monthly drone record

In 2021 NHAI made drone video recording mandatory for national highway projects, every month, at every stage: development, construction, and operation and maintenance (Mobility Outlook, June 2021). Videos compare the current month with the previous one and can be used as evidence in disputes (see NHAI's monthly drone video rule for what each video must show). Contractors and private developers use the same record for their own progress reports and billing.

How a monthly cycle runs

  1. Package plan: chainages, flight path and camera settings are fixed once, so every month is flown the same way.
  2. Control and permissions: DGPS ground control is set, and stretches in yellow or red airspace zones are cleared before the flight date.
  3. Flight: DATA PKT pilots fly the full length of the package.
  4. Processing and analytics: orthomosaics, the comparative video and the analytics are prepared in the office.
  5. Hand-over: files go to the client's monthly progress report and data portal.

How a highway survey like this is done

The monthly cycle above rests on survey engineering that is planned once for each package and then repeated, so that month-on-month comparisons show change on the road rather than change in the survey.

Flight planning

  • Ground sample distance (GSD) first, height second. The GSD is fixed from what the deliverables have to measure, and the flight height follows from the camera: GSD = (sensor width × flight height) ÷ (focal length × image width in pixels). Our GSD calculator does the arithmetic.
  • Corridor flight lines. A highway is a strip, not a block. A single strip of photos is weak geometry; two or more overlapping parallel strips along the alignment, covering the carriageway, shoulders, service roads and right of way, with short cross-strips where needed, hold the block together.
  • Terrain following. On grades, embankments and hill sections the ground rises and falls under a fixed-altitude flight, so the GSD changes along the package. Terrain-following flight keeps the height above ground, and therefore the GSD, close to the target.
  • Separate video runs. The comparative video is flown on its own saved route with fixed height, speed and camera pitch, so that the same chainage appears at the same point in every month's video.
  • Airspace. Flights are planned on the DigitalSky platform under the Drone Rules, 2021, and flown by DGCA Remote Pilot Certificate holders on drones with a Unique Identification Number. Stretches in yellow or red zones are cleared before the flight date.

Ground control design for a corridor

  • GCP pairs on alternate sides. Ground control points (GCPs) are laid out along both sides of the alignment, with extra points at the package ends, at major bends, at flyovers and interchanges, and where the grade changes sharply. GCPs in a single line down the centre of a corridor leave the model free to tilt sideways; pairs across the corridor stop that.
  • Permanent marks. On a monthly programme, permanent marks (pillars, or painted marks on kerbs and structures) are re-used every cycle, so that each month is in exactly the same frame.
  • Checkpoints held out. Checkpoints are measured exactly like GCPs but kept out of the adjustment and used only to test the result.
  • Datum and heights. GNSS gives ellipsoidal heights, while design levels on highway drawings are orthometric (mean sea level). Heights are converted with a geoid model and checked on a benchmark. In India the difference between the two can be tens of metres, so a missed geoid step is not a small error.

Processing chain

  1. Field data check: image count, blur, exposure and GNSS logs are checked before the team leaves the stretch.
  2. Image positions: RTK or PPK processing of the drone's GNSS log against a base station or CORS gives the position of each image.
  3. Aerial triangulation (bundle adjustment): tie points are matched between overlapping images, the camera is self-calibrated, and the GCPs fix the block to the ground.
  4. Dense matching: a dense point cloud is built from the oriented images.
  5. DSM and DTM: the digital surface model (DSM) keeps vehicles, trees and structures; the digital terrain model (DTM) is filtered to ground. Earthwork and pavement levels come from the DTM, edited where filtering has left vehicles or plant on the surface.
  6. Orthomosaic and chainage referencing: the orthomosaic is tiled by chainage, and the design centre line is loaded so that every output can be read by chainage and offset.

What a controlled monthly survey can show

  • Layer-wise progress by chainage: each layer of the pavement crust (embankment, subgrade, granular sub-base, wet mix macadam, dense bituminous macadam, bituminous concrete, as specified in the MoRTH Specifications for Road and Bridge Works) and each structure marked complete, in progress or not started.
  • Cross-sections and longitudinal sections compared with the design sections, to show where formation or finished levels are high or low.
  • Volumes: cut and fill between this month's surface and the previous month's, or the design surface, for earthwork and stockpiles.
  • Analytics layers: encroachments, unauthorised openings, potholes and black-spot observations as GIS features that carry their chainage.

How accuracy is reported

Accuracy is reported as root mean square error (RMSE) at independent checkpoints, following the ASPRS Positional Accuracy Standards for Digital Geospatial Data, Edition 2 (2023): horizontal accuracy as RMSEH, and vertical accuracy on open, hard ground as non-vegetated vertical accuracy (NVA). Edition 2 reports RMSE alone, without the confidence-level figures of Edition 1, asks for at least 30 checkpoints for a product accuracy assessment, and includes the error of the checkpoint survey itself in the reported figure.

Absolute accuracy is how close the map is to true coordinates. Relative accuracy is how consistent it is within itself and, on a monthly programme, from one month to the next. Progress and volume figures depend mostly on relative accuracy between months, which is why the same permanent control is reused every cycle.

Getting the best results

  • Fix the package plan once (chainages, flight lines, heights, camera settings) and change it only when the road changes.
  • Put permanent control in at the start of the contract; re-measuring temporary targets every month adds error and cost.
  • Give the survey team the design alignment and design sections in CAD at the start, so outputs come out by chainage and can be compared with design.
  • Agree with the client which surface is used for each measurement: the DTM for earthwork and pavement levels, the DSM for structures and stockpile tops.
  • Keep vertical bias small for volumes: a constant 2 cm error over one hectare changes a volume by 200 m³, whatever the GSD.
  • Record each month's flight conditions (date, time, wind, light) with the data, so differences between months can be explained.
  • Fly the video and the mapping as separate missions; settings that suit one rarely suit the other.

Frequently asked questions

How many kilometres of highway has DATA PKT flown?

More than 3 lakh km of highway data in the last six years, drone imagery and aerial videography combined, for highway authorities and private contractors. In this financial year (2026-27) we have already captured more than 70,000 km, and the count is still rising.

How fast can a drone team cover a highway?

One DATA PKT drone team captures about 35 km of highway imagery for photogrammetry, or about 50 km of aerial video, in a day. We have more than 20 drone pilots, a team of about 22.

What is a DSP in a highway drone programme?

A drone service provider (DSP) flies the highways, sets DGPS ground control, captures imagery and video, and processes the data. A technology service provider (TSP) runs the technology platform the data feeds into. DATA PKT works as a DSP.

What can the analytics find on a highway?

The rate of completion on projects under construction, and on completed highways: encroachments and broken boundaries, unauthorised U-turns and paths cut by the public, potholes, black spots and accident-prone stretches.

Are monthly drone surveys mandatory on national highway projects?

Yes. Since 2021 NHAI has required monthly drone video recording of national highway projects across development, construction, operation and maintenance, uploaded to its Data Lake portal.

Is DATA PKT empanelled with NHAI?

Yes. DATA PKT is empanelled with NHAI in three zones for monthly drone survey work, and has flown highway drone surveys since October 2020.

Why are checkpoints kept out of the processing?

A point used to fix the map cannot also prove it is right. Checkpoints are measured in the same way as ground control points but left out of the adjustment, and the difference between their surveyed and mapped coordinates is the honest accuracy of the survey.

Can monthly drone data be used for earthwork quantities?

Yes, when the survey is controlled for it: the same ground control every month, a ground-filtered terrain model rather than the raw surface, and checked vertical accuracy. Volumes are only as good as the vertical accuracy, so the accuracy report should go with the quantities.
