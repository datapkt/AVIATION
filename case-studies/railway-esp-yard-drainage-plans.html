Case study · Railways

Yard plans and drainage plans for two railway divisions

For two railway divisions, DATA PKT surveyed station yards with drones and DGPS and turned the data into yard plans and drainage designs: 61 plans submitted in eastern India, and drainage design for 32 yards in Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh.

Eastern India30 yard plans and 31 drainage plans, all submitted
Uttar Pradesh and Madhya PradeshDrainage planning and design for 32 yards
MethodDrone survey with DGPS control, CAD drafting, field checks
Contract periodsSix months each

In short: railways need accurate plans of each yard before remodelling or drainage works. Measuring yards on foot beside running lines is slow and risky. DATA PKT flies each yard, builds an orthomosaic and terrain model, and drafts the yard plan or drainage design from it, with critical details checked on the ground.

Eastern India: 30 yard plans and 31 drainage plans

Under two contracts awarded in January 2026, each with a six-month completion period, DATA PKT prepared yard plans for 23 yards, plus 7 more added during the work, and drainage plans for 31 yards, from suburban yards to junctions on the main line. All 30 yard plans and all 31 drainage plans have been submitted.

Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh: drainage design for 32 yards

The contract, awarded in June 2026 for completion in six months, covers planning and design of drainage in 32 existing yards, from small wayside stations to junctions. The railway asked for LiDAR or an equivalent advanced survey method. DATA PKT has completed the ground survey of all 32 yards, and drainage plans are being prepared and submitted for approval.

For each yard the work covers:

  • A survey report with site conditions and levels
  • An estimate of waste water generated in the yard
  • Drainage slopes and the disposal location
  • Manholes and septic tanks with dispersal trenches, sized for the yard, with a cleaning arrangement
  • Layout plans for the railway's approval

How the work runs

  1. Control: surveyors fix DGPS ground control points around the yard.
  2. Flight: the drone captures the whole yard in one short window, with little time spent beside running lines.
  3. Processing: an orthomosaic and terrain model are built and checked against independent checkpoints.
  4. Drafting: tracks, turnouts, platforms, structures, drains and levels are drawn into the yard plan or drainage design.
  5. Field check and approval: critical details are verified on the ground, and plans are revised until approved.

Why drones are faster

A conventional ground survey with DGPS and total station measures one point at a time, so a team covers a small area each day and a long corridor takes weeks. A DATA PKT drone team captures around 5 sq km of survey data in a day, which is then mapped and analysed in the office. On a linear corridor such as a highway, one team captures about 35 km of drone imagery for photogrammetry, or about 50 km of aerial video, in a day, and DATA PKT has more than 20 drone pilots (a team of about 22) to put in the field. Ground instruments are still used, but only where they matter most: for the control points that fix the map to real coordinates, for checkpoints that prove the accuracy, and for detail the drone cannot see.

Frequently asked questions

How many railway yard plans has DATA PKT prepared?

For one railway division in eastern India we submitted 30 yard plans and 31 drainage plans. For a second division, in Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, we have surveyed all 32 yards in the contract and are preparing their drainage designs.

What does a railway yard drainage plan include?

Ground levels and slopes, the estimated waste water from the yard, the disposal location, and the design of drains, manholes and septic tanks with dispersal trenches, with a cleaning arrangement.

Why survey railway yards with drones?

A drone captures a whole yard in one short window, so staff spend far less time on or beside running lines, and the orthomosaic and terrain model stay on file for later questions.
