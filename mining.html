Stage 1: Exploration and Discovery stage

Mineral exploration is the search for evidence of mineralisation in the surrounding rock. Geological information from many sites is combined and projected across a wider area to build a geological picture. Exploration of new regions grows with the demand for resources, which is expected to rise by 60% by the end of 2030.

Drone surveys have changed how mineral exploration is carried out.

Drone surveys provide a base image of the region's geology at the start of exploration. Each later stage of work in the area builds on and relates back to this initial dataset, making exploration more efficient and accurate.

Stage 2: Development Stage

Using a drone, DATA PKT assists in optimising the following processes throughout the mining development phase:-

Haulage Road Optimization

A haul road is a type of road in the mining industry used to transport heavy and bulky materials by haul trucks. It is sometimes referred to as a haulage road or a haul track.

Material hauling accounts for a significant portion of a mine's expenses. Well-designed and maintained haul roads make transport safer and directly reduce cycle times, fuel use, tyre costs and overall cost per tonne moved.

Drones support haul road work through accurate mapping, time-critical surveys and quick road inspections, helping manage road maintenance over the long term.

Surveying and Mapping

Conventional surveying needs careful measurement, planning and preparation, which slows data collection. A drone survey gathers the same data in much less time and needs half the field labour of a conventional survey.

Tailings dam monitoring

Tailings dams are common at metallurgical and coal mines. Built of earth, they can cover several square kilometres and hold the contaminated by-products of mining. Monitoring them by drone means fewer staff need to enter unsafe areas, as the survey is flown from a line-of-sight distance, away from harmful chemicals and dust.

Stage 3: Production Stage

Stockpile management and calculation

Stockpile management reduces environmental impact and supports regulatory compliance in mining and construction. Covering, grading and vegetation control sediment run-off from soil stockpiles and protect nearby waterways. It matters most for erodible materials, where it prevents soil loss, contamination and hazards to surrounding areas.

DATA PKT measures stockpiles by drone and shares the results with stakeholders, giving a clear record of inventory and reducing waste. Regular quarry surveys show changes early, so issues can be addressed before they affect safety or output. Accurate volumetric measurements improve planning and forecasting.

Stage 4: Land Reclamation

Reclamation of mined land is the process of restoring it to the condition it was in before mining. When you restore land, you are minimizing the negative environmental consequences of mining. Reforestation and new development are the most typical methods of reclamation.

Drone surveys track extraction progress and material movement, showing how much work remains and when reclamation can begin. Repeat aerial surveys are a quick and reliable way to collect this data.

Benefits of using drones in the mining sector:-

Site Safety Management

Mining sites have unstable sections and rugged terrain where safety comes first. Drones capture photogrammetric data of mines that is processed into digital elevation and surface models, supporting better site management and task planning.


Automated Inspection

Automated drone flights can inspect blast sites without putting people at risk, and reduce the manpower needed for mine inspection. The data collected is processed into orthomosaic maps that give an up-to-date view of large areas.


Site Monitoring And Mapping

DATA PKT drones monitor mine sites with high-quality sensors, giving cost-effective access to current data. UAVs can monitor deep tunnels that cannot be inspected manually, helping with site surveillance and the detection of leaks that could endanger people, so corrective action can be taken quickly.


Mine Survey Using Lidar

LiDAR (light detection and ranging) surveys of mine pits produce accurate topographic maps of mining operations. Drone-mounted LiDAR gives precise data on embankment slopes and unstable ground, while surveyors stay clear of difficult terrain.


Volume Calculation

Volume calculations show the cost and efficiency of machines and mining methods. Installing RTK-GPS equipment on site can put surveyors at risk, so drone-derived digital terrain models (DTM) are used to calculate volumes instead.


Deliverables

Detailed Project Reporting (DPR)

We prepare detailed project reports for mining projects from drone survey data, covering terrain mapping, stockpile volumes and ongoing site monitoring.

DTM

We produce Digital Terrain Models (DTM) from drone surveys of mine sites. The elevation data supports terrain mapping, volume calculations and site planning.

Stockpile Volume Measurement

Drones measure stockpile volumes of ore, coal or aggregates quickly and accurately. The figures support inventory management, production planning and financial reporting.

Orthomosaic

We create orthomosaics of mine sites by stitching drone-captured aerial images in photogrammetry software. They provide detailed, accurate imagery for planning, monitoring and quality checks.

DSM

We produce Digital Surface Models (DSM) from drone surveys of mine sites, used for terrain mapping, stockpile volume measurement and site planning.

Aerial Photogrammetry and Videography

We provide drone photography and videography of mining projects, giving a visual record for planning, monitoring and communication with stakeholders.

Topographical Survey

We carry out drone topographic surveys tied to DGPS ground control for mining projects. The terrain data supports resource estimation, site planning and day-to-day operations.

Blast Monitoring and Fragmentation Analysis

We use drones to monitor blasting and analyse rock fragmentation, improving safety and helping raise productivity.

Lidar Survey

LiDAR surveys of mine sites give detailed topographic data for pit planning, infrastructure design and safety assessment, supporting sound construction and maintenance practice.

Pit and Highwall Inspections

We use drones to inspect open pits and highwalls, helping assess slope stability and site safety.

Frequently asked questions

How are stockpile volumes measured with drones?

The drone maps the stockpiles and the software builds a 3D surface. Volumes are calculated against a base surface, which is faster and safer than walking the pile with a survey instrument.

How often should a mine be surveyed by drone?

Many mines fly monthly or quarterly so that excavation, dumping and stock figures can be compared period by period. The frequency depends on how fast the site changes and reporting needs.

Can drones map active pits safely?

Yes. The drone collects data from the air, so surveyors do not need to approach unstable faces or work near moving equipment.

What mining outputs are typical?

Orthomosaics, terrain models, contours, pit and dump boundaries, volume reports, haul road profiles and change-detection maps between surveys.
