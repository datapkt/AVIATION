About DATA PKT

Editorial standards: how we write our guides

How the guides, case studies and service pages on datapkt.com are written, sourced, dated and corrected.

By the DATA PKT team · Published 25 November 2026

In short: our guides are written by the DATA PKT team from our survey, GIS and inspection work. Facts and figures from outside sources link to the source, standards are named by number, and company figures are only those the company has approved for publication. Clients are named only where they have allowed it. Each guide shows its published date, and an updated date when its content changes. Nothing on the site is paid for by a third party. To report an error, email consult@datapkt.com.

Who writes the guides

The guides in Insights are written by the DATA PKT team: the pilots, surveyors, GIS analysts and engineers who run our projects. Each guide carries a team byline, such as "By the DATA PKT survey and GIS engineering team". We do not publish the names of individual staff on the website.

The guides answer questions clients ask us before a survey starts: which method to use, how accurate the result will be, what the deliverables are and what to put in the scope. Where a guide explains how we do something, it describes how our own projects run.

How we cite sources

Facts and figures that come from outside DATA PKT, such as a rule from the drone regulations, a value from a standard, a research result or a manufacturer's specification, are linked to where they come from. Most guides end with a numbered Sources list, and the figure in the text points to its entry, for example [1]. Shorter guides that rely on one or two sources link them in the text.

We prefer primary sources: the Gazette or ministry release, the standard itself, the published study or the manufacturer's own page.

How we name standards

Standards are named by their number, with the edition or year where it matters, so readers can find the exact document. For example: ASPRS Positional Accuracy Standards for Digital Geospatial Data, Edition 2 (2023), and IEC TS 62446-3:2017 for thermographic inspection of PV plants. Indian standards and circulars are named by their IS number or circular number and date.

Company figures and client names

Figures about DATA PKT itself, such as kilometres of highway data captured, team size or plans delivered, are limited to figures the company has approved for publication.

Client names appear on the site only where we are permitted to name them. Our case studies describe the work, the method and the outputs; they do not name the client, division or site unless we are permitted to, and their text does not give contract values or site coordinates. Project images have project names and GPS coordinates blurred or removed before they are published. Where a client cannot be named, the case study says so; one, for example, lists its client as "Private client (name withheld)".

Dates and updates

Each guide shows the date it was first published. When we change the content of a guide, for example to add a section, correct a figure or reflect a new rule, we update it and show the "Updated" date beside the published date. The same dates are recorded in the page's structured data.

No paid placements or sponsored content

We do not accept payment to feature, link to or recommend a product, manufacturer or company in our guides. There is no sponsored content on datapkt.com. Where a guide names equipment, it is to explain a specification.

Why we do not publish prices

We do not publish prices or price bands. A survey's cost depends on its location, the area or corridor length, the accuracy needed and the deliverables, so we quote per project. Our survey cost page explains what sets the cost; to get a quote, send us the site and the outputs you need.

How to report an error

If you find a mistake on any page, or a source that has changed, email consult@datapkt.com with the page address and what needs correcting. We check the point against the source and, where the page is wrong, correct it and change its "Updated" date.
