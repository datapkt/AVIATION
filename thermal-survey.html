Solar plant drone thermography and inspection

In short: we carry out solar plant drone thermography and thermal surveys of substations and roofs with radiometric cameras. Solar plants are flown in steady sunlight with the plant running. The report classifies each hotspot, cell, diode or string fault and tags it to its place in the plant, so the O&M team can find and fix it (what the report should include). Our thermal team includes a thermographer certified to ISO 9712 Level 2, and our power-sector clients include NTPC and Adani.

Reporting and Recommendations

Each report lists every defect with its class and its position on the plant layout, with the thermal and visual images and the temperature difference to healthy modules, and a summary by block or inverter so repairs can be planned. See what a solar thermography report should include.

When to inspect

  • At commissioning or handover, to record the condition of every module before the plant changes hands or the warranty period runs on.
  • In routine O&M cycles, to track changes over time, find new faults and check that earlier repairs worked.
  • After storms or a drop in performance, when hail, wind or an unexplained fall in yield points to damage that needs locating.
  • Before a warranty claim, so each defect is documented with its class, its position and radiometric images.

What a solar thermal inspection covers

We use radiometric thermal cameras that record thermal images and video with a temperature value for every pixel, at centimetre-level detail. On a solar plant the inspection covers every module and string, from single cells up to whole blocks, and we support your O&M team through interpretation of the results.

Solar panel thermal imaging survey

With radiometric thermal imaging, a drone survey finds faults at cell level within a panel, panel level within a sub-array and array level within a block, faster and more safely than manual inspection. See our guide to solar thermography reports.

The ability to detect and diagnose issues with such precision enables maintenance teams to promptly enhance energy output and prevent further damage. By swiftly identifying performance faults, teams can take proactive measures to mitigate potential losses and ensure optimal operational efficiency of solar installations.

To facilitate the identification of performing panels or sub-arrays, we offer raw thermal radiometric imagery for processing. Alternatively, our expert team can handle the thermal data analysis, producing detailed hotspot reports integrated with GPS data. This comprehensive approach ensures that our clients receive actionable insights tailored to their specific needs, supporting informed decision-making and proactive maintenance strategies.

How we inspect a solar plant

We fly to the conditions and requirements of IEC TS 62446-3, the technical specification for outdoor infrared thermography of PV modules and plants. A thermal image taken outside these conditions can hide faults or show faults that are not there.

  1. Site assessment and flight plan. We agree the requirements with the plant team, assess the site for areas of interest, risks and safety needs, and plan the flights for weather, flight height and coverage.
  2. Plant running, conditions stable. The plant is in normal operation and the flight is made in clear, steady sunlight, because a fault only shows as heat when the modules are producing power.
  3. Irradiance of at least 600 W/m². Irradiance in the plane of the modules is checked before and during the flight. If it drops below the minimum, flying stops until it recovers.
  4. Wind and cloud within limits. Cloud cover of no more than 2 okta (2/8 of the sky) and wind of no more than 4 Beaufort (about 28 km/h), so that passing shadows and wind cooling do not mask or mimic faults.
  5. Resolution of about 3 cm per pixel. The flight height is set so that each thermal pixel covers about 3 cm of the module, which puts roughly five pixels across each cell.
  6. Viewing angle above 30° to the module plane. At shallower angles the glass reflects the sky and the sun, and the temperature reading is no longer reliable.
  7. Radiometric camera. Every pixel carries a temperature value, so each anomaly can be measured against healthy modules and compared from one year to the next.
  8. Certified personnel. IEC TS 62446-3 expects the thermographer to hold a recognised certification. Our thermal team includes a thermographer certified to ISO 9712 Level 2.
  9. Processing and analysis. After the flight, our team processes the raw thermal data into thermal maps and analyses them for hotspots and other anomalies. Each one is classified and tagged to its place in the plant for the report.

What thermal imaging does not show

Thermography finds faults that make heat. It does not reliably find cell cracks that have not yet cut a cell off; electroluminescence (EL) imaging shows those. It estimates, but does not measure, the power a string is losing; an IV-curve trace measures it. And it cannot see inside combiner boxes or inverters, or tell whether a connector is loose before it heats up; those need physical checks on the ground.

A good inspection plan uses the drone to find and map every fault, then sends the ground team only where the drone points. Read the full comparison in drone vs manual solar panel inspection.

Frequently asked questions

What is a drone thermal survey?

A drone thermal survey uses an infrared camera on a drone to measure surface temperature across a site. Faults that change temperature, such as failing solar cells, loose electrical joints or water ingress, become visible in the thermal image.

When should a solar thermal inspection be flown?

In clear, steady sunlight with the plant operating, usually around midday. The widely used standard for aerial thermography of PV plants, IEC TS 62446-3, sets a minimum irradiance of 600 W/m² on the modules.

What defects can thermal drones find in a solar plant?

Hot spots and cell cracks, bypass diode faults, open or disconnected strings, junction box overheating, potential induced degradation patterns, and soiling or shading.

What is the difference between radiometric and non-radiometric thermal cameras?

A radiometric camera records a temperature value for every pixel, so each defect can be measured and compared. A non-radiometric camera only shows relative hot and cold areas. Inspection reports need radiometric data.

What does a solar thermography report include?

Each defect with its class, its position on the plant layout, the thermal and visual images, and the temperature difference to healthy modules, with a summary by block or inverter so repairs can be planned.

Does the plant need to shut down for a drone thermal inspection?

No. The plant must be running, because a fault only shows as heat when the modules are producing power. The flight is made during normal operation, in steady sunlight, and is coordinated with the plant team so that no blocks are switched off or curtailed.

Is a drone thermal inspection compliant with IEC TS 62446-3?

Only if it meets the standard's conditions, resolution, personnel and reporting requirements; a drone does not make an inspection compliant by itself. The conditions include irradiance of at least 600 W/m² on the modules, cloud cover of no more than 2 okta, wind of no more than 4 Beaufort and a resolution of about 3 cm per pixel, and the thermographer should hold a recognised certification such as ISO 9712.

What can a drone thermal inspection not find?

Cell cracks that have not yet cut a cell off, which electroluminescence (EL) imaging shows; the exact power a string is losing, which an IV-curve trace measures; and faults inside combiner boxes and inverters, which need a physical check on the ground.
